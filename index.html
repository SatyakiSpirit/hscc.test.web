Understanding Hemophilia

Learn about this inherited bleeding disorder

🔬

What is Hemophilia?

Hemophilia is a rare inherited bleeding disorder where blood doesn't clot normally. It occurs due to deficiency of clotting factors. People with hemophilia bleed longer after injury, surgery, or dental procedures. In severe cases, spontaneous bleeding can occur in joints and muscles.

🧬

Types of Hemophilia

Hemophilia A (Classic): Caused by lack of Factor VIII - most common type (about 80% of cases). Hemophilia B (Christmas Disease): Caused by lack of Factor IX. Von Willebrand Disease (VWD): Most common inherited bleeding disorder, caused by deficiency of Von Willebrand factor.

⚠️

Signs & Symptoms

Excessive bleeding from cuts or injuries. Large or deep bruises. Unusual bleeding after vaccinations. Pain, swelling in joints. Blood in urine or stool. Nosebleeds without known cause. In infants, unexplained irritability. Tightness in joints. Prolonged bleeding from tooth extraction.

Our Activities

Programs and services we offer to our community

🏥

Medical Camps

Regular medical camps organized with specialist hematologists for free check-ups, diagnosis and treatment guidance for hemophilia patients.

💪

Physiotherapy Sessions

Regular physiotherapy sessions to help patients maintain joint mobility, strengthen muscles and prevent joint damage caused by repeated bleeding episodes.

📢

Awareness Programs

Community awareness campaigns, World Hemophilia Day celebrations, school programs, and workshops to educate the public about bleeding disorders.

💊

Factor Distribution

Distribution of clotting factor concentrates to registered patients during emergencies and for prophylaxis treatment to prevent bleeding episodes.

🎓

Educational Support

Scholarships and educational support for children with hemophilia to ensure they don't miss out on education due to their condition.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family Counseling

Psychological counseling and support groups for patients and families to cope with the emotional challenges of living with a chronic bleeding disorder.

⚠️ Bleeding Emergency Guide

What to do when a hemophilia patient starts bleeding

🚨 Immediate Steps

  • Stay calm and reassure the patient
  • Apply RICE method: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation
  • Administer factor concentrate if available and trained
  • Apply direct pressure with clean cloth on external wounds
  • Contact Hemophilia Treatment Center immediately

🏥 When to Go to Hospital

  • Head injury or severe headache after trauma
  • Bleeding that doesn't stop with first aid
  • Bleeding in neck, throat or tongue
  • Severe joint or muscle bleed with swelling
  • Abdominal pain or blood in urine/stool

❌ What NOT to Do

  • Do NOT give Aspirin or Ibuprofen
  • Do NOT apply tourniquet unless life-threatening
  • Do NOT ignore minor bleeds - treat early
  • Do NOT give intramuscular injections
  • Do NOT delay treatment - 'Treat first, diagnose later'

📋 Always Carry

  • Hemophilia patient card/ID at all times
  • Emergency contact numbers list
  • Medical records and treatment history
  • First aid kit with ice pack
  • Factor concentrate if on home treatment

Do's and Don'ts for Patients

✅ Do's

  • ✓ Wear a medical alert bracelet/ID
  • ✓ Exercise regularly - swimming, walking are excellent
  • ✓ Maintain healthy body weight
  • ✓ Take regular prophylaxis as prescribed
  • ✓ Keep dental hygiene excellent to avoid dental procedures
  • ✓ Inform all doctors about your condition
  • ✓ Get vaccinated (subcutaneous route only)
  • ✓ Keep a bleeding diary/record

❌ Don'ts

  • ✗ Don't take Aspirin, Ibuprofen, blood thinners
  • ✗ Don't play contact sports (football, boxing, wrestling)
  • ✗ Don't ignore even minor bleeding
  • ✗ Don't get intramuscular injections
  • ✗ Don't delay treatment when bleeding occurs
  • ✗ Don't undergo surgery without factor replacement
  • ✗ Don't use razor blades - use electric shaver
  • ✗ Don't miss regular check-ups at HTC
